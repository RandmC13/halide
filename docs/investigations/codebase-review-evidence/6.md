# Task 6.1 — Driving the picker GUI as a first-time user

Setup: Xvfb :57, `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=xcb`, `HALIDE_NO_COMPLETION=1`, a scratch `HOME`/profile store,
`--device cpu`, a working copy of 8 frames of Roll16-Testing (`.review/scratch/6/roll`, deleted at
the end). No window manager is running in this sandbox (per the brief) — noted below wherever it
plausibly matters, and one case (close-hang) was specifically checked against that possibility.
Screenshots referenced below are in `.review/gui/`; `roll16-contact-full.jpg` in that folder is
**not mine** (37-frame `Roll16AutoTest` sheet, a concurrent task's artifact) — ignore it.

## What works

The picker is, overall, a genuinely well-designed piece of software and it shows on screen, not
just in the code:

- **The fixed-size, non-scrolling landscape layout (R-100) holds up at every resolution tested**
  (1366x768, 1920x1080, 2560x1440, and 2x HiDPI) — filmstrip, image, right panel and caption are
  all visible with no scrollbar anywhere except the point list, exactly as designed.
  `1-1366x768.png`, `1-1920x1080.png`, `16-2560x1440.png`, `17-hidpi-2x.png`.
- **The negative-to-positive transformation is the single best "wow" moment in the app.**
  Clicking two well-separated neutral points turns a flat orange negative into a correctly
  white-balanced photo in front of you, live. `2-positive-view.png`.
- **The agreement math does exactly what CLAUDE.md says, including the subtle part.** Adding a
  deliberately-bad point (clicking an orange jacket) produced "⚠ Point 4 is furthest from the
  others (CC 9 R)" — even though point 1's own reading was *numerically worse* (CC 24 R). That
  matches the documented "removal leaves the others most consistent, not simply the furthest off"
  rule, confirmed live, not just by reading the code. `2-bad-point-hint.png`.
- **The Details drawer's "Show auto-detected neutral candidates" overlay** renders a sensible green
  mask over plausible neutral surfaces (a wall, trousers) and correctly excludes the orange jacket.
  `2-auto-candidates-overlay.png`.
- **The Develop/Save button is genuinely gated on having a usable calibration**, not just "≥2
  points": three points clustered within 0.05 D stayed a dim, disabled-looking grey; the same three
  points re-picked with real separation (0.89–1.07 D) turned bright red immediately. This is a
  real, working safety rail against saving a degenerate fit, not a cosmetic detail.
  `18-invert-pick.png` vs `18-repick-wider2.png`.
- **The overwrite guard on Save is real and matches the CLI/GUI comparison CLAUDE.md calls for**:
  saving over an existing name pops "A profile named 'Test-Profile-1' already exists -" with the
  button itself relabelled "Overwrite". `2-save-dup-overwrite-prompt.png`.
- **Reopening a profile, and re-finding a moved roll, both work as documented.** `--profile
  NAME` restores every point on its correct frame (`3-reopen-profile.png`); moving the roll folder
  and reopening produces the exact "was made from … moved or deleted" dialog with "Find roll…" /
  "Continue without", and "Continue without" leaves the points marked `(missing)` but still
  counted in the fit, with a plain-language status line. `4-roll-not-found.png`,
  `4-after-continue-without.png`.
- **The contact sheet is the best-looking screen in the tool.** Both the instant draft and the
  full-quality 8-frame sheet render the real film/darkroom identity CLAUDE.md describes — black
  rebate, orange edge print, "INVERTED BY HALIDE", per-frame barcode/grade caption — convincingly,
  not just "dark theme with borders". `6-proof-window-opening.png`, `6-proof-full-quality.png`.
- **`invert --pick` is correctly the same picker minus the roll furniture** (no filmstrip, no
  contact-sheet button, red button reads "Develop"), and produced a real, correct output file in
  3.2s with the expected provenance line printed to the terminal. `18-invert-pick.png`.
- **Esc and Delete both do the right, unsurprising thing**: Esc dismisses the Save dialog with no
  side effects; Delete on a selected point removes it, renumbers the rest, and brings back the
  "Add a third point…" hint. `12-esc-on-save-dialog.png`, `10-after-delete-key.png`.
- **"Clear all" is guarded by a confirmation dialog** ("Remove all 3 point(s) and start the
  calibration again?"), not an instant destructive action.

## New findings

### 6-A: `_WINDOW_MAX = QSize(1400, 900)` caps the window well under "~55% x 70%" on large screens
- Severity: S5 polish / doc mismatch
- Where: `src/halide/gui/main_window.py:74-75,102` (`_WINDOW_MIN`/`_WINDOW_MAX`/`_compute_window_size`)
- Evidence: at 2560x1440 the window measures exactly 1400x900 px (confirmed by scanning the
  screenshot's pixel rows/columns: last non-background pixel at row 5 is x=1399, at column 5 is
  y=899), i.e. 54.7% x 62.5% of the screen — not 55% x 70%. R-100/CLAUDE.md document the floor
  (900x700) but not this ceiling, so on any screen at or above ~2545x1286 the window stops growing
  and the "~55% x 70%" description quietly stops being true. Not a bug (still fixed-size, still no
  scrolling — the actual intent of R-100 holds) but worth updating the requirement/doc text, or
  reconsidering the cap for very large monitors where 1400x900 leaves most of the screen unused.
- Confidence: confirmed

### 6-B: Confirms 2.6-2 — contact sheet staleness tracking breaks after the first rebuild
- Severity: S2 bug (matches 2.6-2's rating)
- Reproduced live end to end: built a sheet, added a 4th point (sheet correctly showed "Points or
  print settings have changed since this sheet was built." + a "Rebuild contact sheet" button),
  clicked Rebuild (`8-rebuild-clicked-immediately.png` shows the fresh draft appearing), then added
  a 5th point. The rebuilt, still-open sheet **never showed the staleness banner again** — it sat
  on "Full quality · 8 frames" with no warning and no Rebuild button, even though the calibration
  had changed a second time (`9-second-change-check-stale.png`). This is exactly the `self._proof`
  Qt deferred-signal race 2.6-2 predicted from reading the code; Phase 6 now confirms it live,
  including the specific repro path ("build, edit, rebuild, edit again").
- Confidence: confirmed

### 6-C: Closing the calibrate picker's window never quits the process — a real, severe hang (new; also bears on 2.6-3)
- Severity: S1 bug
- Where: `src/halide/gui/main_window.py:1051-1058` (`MainWindow.closeEvent`), `src/halide/gui/app.py:38` (`app.exec()`)
- Evidence: launched `halide calibrate <roll>`, waited for it to fully finish loading (all 8
  filmstrip thumbnails in, coverage bar computed, idle for 10s — `15-fully-loaded-before-close.png`),
  then sent a normal `WM_DELETE_WINDOW` close request (`xdotool windowclose`, the same protocol a
  real window manager's titlebar/Alt-F4 sends — not a forced kill). The window visibly disappeared
  (gone from the window list; `MainWindow.closeEvent`'s own `self.hide()` ran), but the **process
  kept running**: `py-spy dump` on the still-alive PID showed the main thread simply sitting back
  inside `app.exec()` at `halide/gui/app.py:38` — i.e. `closeEvent` had already returned and the
  Qt event loop was idle, not blocked waiting on anything. It was still running, doing nothing (all
  threads in `do_sys_poll`, ~0 additional CPU time), **119+ seconds** later, with its
  `resource_tracker`/`forkserver` children still alive too (~450 MB RSS held the whole time).
  Repeated with a *fresh-loaded, fully idle* window (no pending loads at all) and got the identical
  result within 6+ seconds of closing — so this is not specific to "closing mid-load" as CLAUDE.md's
  "up to ~2.7s" note describes; the app doesn't reliably quit on close at all in this environment.
  Likely mechanism (inferred, not verified against Qt's C++ source): `closeEvent` calls
  `self.hide()` **manually, before** `super().closeEvent(event)` runs. Qt's normal
  `quitOnLastWindowClosed` bookkeeping expects to observe the widget's own visibility transition as
  part of its own close machinery; hiding it early, out of band, plausibly causes Qt to never
  register "last visible top-level window closed" and so never calls `quit()`.
  **Ruled out as a pure no-WM/xdotool artifact**: the *same* sandbox, same `xdotool`, same missing
  WM cleanly exits `halide invert --pick` after clicking "Develop" (verified in this session —
  process gone immediately, no zombie) because that path quits via its own explicit `QEventLoop`
  rather than relying on `quitOnLastWindowClosed`. That the interactive `calibrate` path is the one
  that hangs, and the `--pick` path (same toolkit, same display, same window-close plumbing) is
  not, points at the app-level quit logic specifically, not the test environment.
  This directly contradicts the CLAUDE.md decision "closing waits (no timeout) for every loader
  thread... down to ~2.7s after closing during the first load" (implying bounded, eventual exit)
  and R-108 ("closing any window stops its workers at once"). A real user closing the calibration
  picker from a desktop icon would see the window disappear and reasonably assume the app quit,
  while a Python process and two child processes keep consuming memory until manually killed.
- Suggestion: add `QApplication.setQuitOnLastWindowClosed(True)` explicitly (belt-and-braces) and/or
  call `QApplication.instance().quit()` at the end of `closeEvent` when this is the last window, and
  add a regression test that launches the picker headless, closes it, and asserts the process exits
  within a few seconds.
- Confidence: confirmed (reproduced twice, with `py-spy` evidence; root cause is inferred from
  reading `closeEvent`, not from Qt internals, but the observed behaviour itself is solid)

### 6-D: A transient but real mis-rendered label — "NEUTRAL POINTS" briefly paints as "JTRAL POINTS" while the "Roll not found" dialog is open
- Severity: S5 polish (likely a Qt/Xvfb repaint quirk, flagged rather than filed hard)
- Where: `src/halide/gui/main_window.py:438` (`points_title = QLabel("NEUTRAL POINTS  (?)")`)
- Evidence: while the "Roll not found" modal was open, the panel's "NEUTRAL POINTS (?)" header
  reproducibly rendered as "JTRAL POINTS (?)" — confirmed at native resolution (no scaling
  artifact), confirmed on a second independent screenshot taken a moment later (not a one-frame
  capture glitch), and confirmed **gone** (back to "NEUTRAL POINTS (?)") the instant the dialog was
  dismissed via "Continue without" and the window repainted. `4-crop-native.png` vs
  `4-crop-after-continue.png`. A close pixel crop shows the stray glyph rendered in blue/orange/teal
  — colours matching the point-marker palette, not the label's own muted "dim" colour — consistent
  with a stale/partial repaint (old pixels under the label not fully cleared) rather than a font or
  string bug. Given this project runs headless Xvfb+xcb here, this may well be specific to that
  software-rendering path rather than something a user on a real compositor would ever see — noted
  rather than escalated, but it means the label's paint region isn't being fully invalidated
  somewhere in this code path, which is worth a second look on real hardware.
- Confidence: confirmed (reproduced), root cause unconfirmed

### 6-E: No visible keyboard-focus indicator anywhere in the picker
- Severity: S4 accessibility/polish
- Where: whole-window (theme/QSS), observed via `11-tab-focus.png`
- Evidence: pressing Tab three times from a fresh point-list selection produced no visible focus
  ring, border-highlight, or other indication on any button, field, or list row — the screenshot is
  pixel-identical in the relevant areas to the unfocused state. A keyboard-only user (or someone
  just trying to tab through the Save dialog) has no way to see where focus is.
- Confidence: confirmed (absence of any visual change after Tab), though not exhaustively — it's
  possible some specific widget does show a focus rect that these three Tab-stops didn't reach.

### 6-F: Status line text is not wrapped or elided and can run off the window's right edge
- Severity: S5 polish
- Where: `src/halide/gui/main_window.py:1048-1049` (`self.status_label`)
- Evidence: after a successful save, the status line read "Saved calibration profile
  'Test-Profile-1' to /home/sam/Documents/halide/.review/scratch/6/home/.config" — cut off mid-path
  at the window's edge with no ellipsis or wrap, because `status_label` has no `setWordWrap(True)`.
  Any real profile path will typically be longer than this test path (a real `~/.config/halide/
  profiles/...`), so this will regularly truncate mid-word in normal use. `2-save-success2.png` was
  captured before deletion but the same behaviour is visible on the "Roll not found" status line too.
- Confidence: confirmed

### 6-G: "measuring the roll…" is left showing indefinitely once there is no roll to measure
- Severity: S5 polish
- Where: `src/halide/gui/main_window.py` (coverage panel's roll-estimate placeholder text)
- Evidence: after "Continue without" on a missing roll, the coverage panel is left permanently
  reading "measuring the roll…" (a static string, not an animated spinner) even though there is no
  roll and nothing is being measured — `4-after-continue-without.png`. A user could reasonably read
  this as the app being stuck/loading forever.
- Confidence: confirmed

## Confirming/refuting the named 2.6 findings

- **2.6-2 (contact sheet staleness breaks after first rebuild): CONFIRMED live.** See 6-B above —
  reproduced the exact "build, edit, rebuild, edit again" sequence 2.6-2 asked Phase 6 to check.
- **2.6-3 (rebuild blocks the main window's UI thread): Not independently confirmed as a visible
  freeze.** The 8-frame test roll's old `ProofWindow` closed and the new one's draft appeared with
  no perceptible delay in screenshots taken immediately after clicking Rebuild
  (`8-rebuild-clicked-immediately.png`). The blocking code path (`self._proof.close()` calling
  `QThread.wait()` with no timeout, called synchronously from `_on_proof` on the GUI thread) is
  still there and unambiguous on reading, and a larger roll (37 frames, more/slower workers to tear
  down) would very plausibly show it — this pass's roll was just too small/fast to force it
  visibly. Left as **likely**, unchanged from 2.6's own confidence.
- **2.6-4 (unbounded concurrent full-resolution filmstrip loads): Not exercised this pass.** Did
  not do rapid multi-click filmstrip scrubbing on the 8-frame roll; nothing observed here confirms
  or refutes it. Left as **likely**, per 2.6's own confidence, and worth a dedicated pass with the
  full 37-frame roll and a memory monitor if this gets prioritized.
- **2.6-7 (no HiDPI/devicePixelRatio awareness): Code-level gap confirmed present (no
  `setDevicePixelRatio`/`devicePixelRatio` anywhere in `gui/`, verified again), but the predicted
  visible softness was NOT clearly confirmed at `QT_SCALE_FACTOR=2` on 2560x1440** — a close crop of
  the negative preview (`17-crop-sharpness.png`) still shows fine rock texture detail, not an
  obviously blurred/mushy image. `QT_SCALE_FACTOR` performs Qt-level integer scaling (real
  re-layout, not a stretched bitmap), which likely masks most of what 2.6-7 anticipated; a true
  HiDPI backing-store display (native `devicePixelRatio` reporting 2.0 via a Wayland/macOS
  compositor, not the blunt env-var override) would be a better test and wasn't available here.
  Downgraded to **likely, visually inconclusive** rather than confirmed or refuted.

## Newcomer test

Without the README, the empty states and captions carry enough information to proceed at every
step tried: "No points yet — click something that was white, grey or black in the real scene."
(first launch), the negative-brightness-reversal caption (stated in plain words, not jargon), "Add
a third point to see how well your points agree." (nudges toward using the tool's real feature),
and the Save dialog's plain "Name this calibration:". The GUI's own vocabulary lines up with the
CLI's (per 2.6's own strings-comparison table: "fitted"/"pinned" identical in both). The one place
a newcomer could stumble: the third slot in the Negative/Positive row is a plain-text status label
(`source_note`, e.g. "Raw negative" or "Your calibration · N points"), not a button — it sits
directly beside two real pill-shaped buttons with no visual distinction beyond the missing pill
background, so a first click there (expecting a third view mode) does nothing and isn't obviously
explained as "not a button" rather than "broken button". Minor, not filed as a separate finding
given the risk is low (no pill styling at all, so most users will not read it as clickable).
